Introduction

The black capuchin monkey (Sapajus nigritus) inhabits the Atlantic Forest, including forest fragments and production landscapes like pine plantations. Their ability to thrive in disturbed environments makes them a key species for understanding primate adaptation to human-altered habitats.

Bark-Stripping Behavior

Capuchins engage in seasonal bark-stripping of pine trees, which creates tension with forestry producers due to the economic loss caused by tree damage.

Trophic Ecology

The capuchin diet includes fruits and insects, but bark-stripping becomes more intense seasonally. All hypothesis proposed to explain the underlying causes of this behavior aren't supported by evidence, difficulting proper management.

Movement Study

To better understand the ecology of capuchins within production landscapes we set out to study movement and space use of groups living in both pine plantations and native continuous forests.

Conflict & Solutions

To reduce damage to plantations, distraction feeding platforms have been proposed to redirect capuchin bark-stripping behavior.
